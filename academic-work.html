Play and the transition to Key Stage 1

An interview-based study exploring how play-based learning can support children's emotional wellbeing and learning as they move from Reception into Year 1. Drawing on interviews with three primary teachers, it considers child-led and teacher-guided play, transition planning, relationships with families, and curriculum constraints. It is relevant to primary education because it shows how thoughtful play provision can reduce anxiety, strengthen emotional regulation and friendships, and help bridge the gap between EYFS and KS1.

Radical education essay
BA Education, Society and Culture
2026

The Woodland Studio: A Vision for Democratic, Play-Based and Eco-Conscious Education

Presented as a prospectus for an imagined school, this paper develops a practical vision of radical education centred on democratic decision-making, play, creative agency and ecological responsibility. Drawing on thinkers and models including Freire, hooks, Froebel, Summerhill, Sudbury and forest school practice, it examines how children can help shape their learning while also considering structure, accessibility, social inequality and assessment. It is relevant to primary education because it connects child-centred theory with concrete classroom and whole-school approaches that support pupil voice, inclusion, environmental awareness and meaningful learning.

Children with Disabilities essay
BA Education Studies

Considering How the Arts Contribute to the Teaching and Learning of Pupils with Severe and Profound Learning Difficulties

This essay critically examines how music, theatre and visual arts can support pupils with severe and profound learning difficulties. It considers arts-based teaching and therapeutic approaches as routes to communication, sensory engagement, motor development, self-expression, social interaction and agency, while also addressing limited evidence, difficulties measuring outcomes, and the risk of adults misinterpreting or dominating pupils' responses. It is relevant to education because it highlights the value of accessible, multisensory and person-centred learning experiences that recognise diverse ways of communicating and participating.
