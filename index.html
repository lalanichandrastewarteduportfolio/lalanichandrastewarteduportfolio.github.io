02 / ABOUT

I believe learning begins
with belonging.

While I loved learning as a curious child, I also remember how isolating school could feel when I struggled to understand a concept or connect with my peers. Those experiences inspired me to become the patient and encouraging teacher I sometimes needed, who can make difficult ideas accessible while supporting social, emotional and academic development.

My commitment to teaching has been strengthened through experience in nurseries, nannying and primary schools in the UK. During a Reception/Kindergarten placement, I supported phonics and mathematics sessions, led small reading groups and helped children practise letter formation. I saw how transforming a repetitive task into an imaginative game could reduce frustration and guide a child’s enthusiasm for learning. My nursery and nannying experience similarly showed me the value of child-led play, outdoor exploration, creativity and giving children meaningful choices.

Having attended four different primary schools while growing up in Australia, I am particularly interested in how schools support children through transitions. This interest has shaped my academic research into play-based learning and the movement from Reception into Key Stage 1.

My Education Studies degree at UCL deepened my understanding of the social, political and psychological influences on children’s education, including the experiences of pupils with Special educational needs.

I aspire to become a reflective, child-centred teacher who creates a classroom in which pupils feel understood, capable and excited to learn.
